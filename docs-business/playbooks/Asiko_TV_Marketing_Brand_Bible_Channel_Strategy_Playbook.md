# Reusable Marketing Plan, Brand Bible & Channel Strategy Playbook — extracted for Asiko TV

*Source: the "OnAir TV" / "Telairvision" brand-and-marketing package inside the Telairvision archive — `OTT marketing plan.pptx`, `DTH marketing plan_updated 04.04.23.pptx`, and `Telairvision channels_ strategy_updated.pdf` (all authored by Efe Obiomah for the Airband/Telairvision engagement), plus the `Deliverables for Broadcast Team` operational email. None of this is Asiko-specific — it's the strategic skeleton from the earlier venture, stripped and reframed for reuse. Treat every number, competitor name, and date as illustrative, not gospel — validate against Asiko's actual budget, market, and audience before locking anything in.*

**A note on why this is safe to reuse:** the underlying strategic *frameworks* here — stakeholder mapping, persona-building, channel positioning, go-to-market phasing — are generic marketing methodology, not anyone's proprietary IP. The specific Telairvision/OnAir brand names, colours, and channel names are not (see the earlier lessons-learned doc on Clause 9 IP assignment) — so this extraction rebuilds the *structure* for Asiko rather than copying the old brand's actual names, colours or copy.

## 1. Brand Bible framework (structure to fill in for Asiko)

The old deck's brand-definition approach is a clean, fast template. For Asiko TV, this would draw on the identity you've already locked (tagline "The Soul of Africa" / "One Africa. One Market. One Destination.", palette Asiko Black/Deep Charcoal/Soul Gold/Light Gold/Asiko White, Sora + Inter type):

**Brand personality statement** — one or two sentences that pin the tone (e.g., the old deck's: *"[Brand] is bubbly and bold. It is premium yet down-to-earth, approachable and people-oriented."*) followed by a short rationale tying each claim to something real about the audience or heritage.

**Brand archetypes** — 3 one-word/one-phrase pillars (old deck used: *Approachable & down-to-earth / Affordable premium / Trustworthy*). Asiko's equivalent might draw from "community TV," "diaspora connection," "own your bag" — worth condensing into 3 archetypes the same way.

**Target audience tiers** — Primary / Secondary / Tertiary, each with age range, life stage, media habits, and what they emotionally want from the platform. The old deck's structure:
- *Primary:* age band + life stage + what they watch/read/follow + what they do socially (a full day-in-the-life sketch, not just demographics).
- *Secondary:* a different life stage with a distinct pain point (their example: a busy 35–55-year-old who "believes DStv is exploiting Nigerians" and wants something cheap that keeps the family happy).
- *Tertiary:* diaspora / heritage-seekers — directly relevant to Asiko since diaspora *is* your primary audience, so this tier structure should probably be inverted for you (diaspora primary, homeland secondary).

**Content acquisition guidelines** — a short, explicit checklist defining what counts as "premium" content, so curation is consistent across a large channel bouquet. The old deck's checklist (adapt the same shape for movies, series, and studio/talk shows):
- Theatrical release or festival recognition
- High production values (cinematography, editing, set/costume design)
- At least one recognizable/A-list name attached
- Relatable, universal themes (love, betrayal, family, coming-of-age)
- Clean subtitling, no grammatical errors
- For talk/variety shows: strong set design, an engaging host, socially/newsworthy topics, good production values

This is a genuinely reusable, one-page "what do we accept onto the platform" policy — worth writing one for Asiko before content partner onboarding scales up, especially given Blessing Agofure's offer of 1,000 movies in a month.

## 2. Per-channel strategy template (proven structure, 13 channels' worth of examples)

Every channel in the old deck follows the same five-part template. This maps directly onto Asiko's "2 channels per country" / genre-based bouquet approach:

1. **Channel description & target audience** (age band, urban/suburban/diaspora, core vs. wider audience)
2. **Genre**
3. **Positioning map** — plot the channel on a quality (low↔high) vs. price (low↔high) grid against 1–2 named real competitors
4. **Channel strategy** — explicitly state:
   - Market position: *leader* (first-of-its-kind, no direct competitor), *challenger* (going after an established competitor), or *niche* (small addressable segment, mainstream ambition on a timeline)
   - Goal (usually time-bound, e.g. "market leader within 3 years")
   - Content mix
   - **Channel differentiator** — the one sentence that says why a viewer picks this over the incumbent
5. **Content ideation prompts** — a short brainstorm list of specific shows/personalities/formats the target audience would want, used to brief content/production teams

## 3. Go-to-market marketing plan (structure + reusable tactics)

The two decks together describe a full three-phase go-to-market motion:

### Phase 1 — Stakeholder engagement
- Map stakeholders on a power/interest grid: advertising industry bodies, viewers, filmmakers/content providers, distributors, and platform staff/consultants all sit in different quadrants and need different conversion tactics (e.g., "convert to key participators," "convert to advocates via product education and rewards").
- Anchor event: an **industry conference** bringing together the ad/media bodies (their list: MIPAN, ADVAN, ARCON, AAAN, EXMAN) with a stated agenda — welcome, promo reel, "why us / what's different," "what's in it for advertisers," Q&A, close. **This matches your own stated plan to pitch Asiko to MIPAN, ADVAN, and EXMAN** — the old deck gives you a ready-made event runbook (venue tier, guest count, format, pre-requisites: test transmission concluded, promo video, website live, branding assets, brochures).
- Partner-facing enablement: joint technical/content training sessions with infrastructure partners, ending with branded merchandise handoff.

### Phase 2 — Drive tune-in from the existing/nearest base
- Define one existing distribution base to convert first (their case: SES's existing satellite customer base; your equivalent might be an existing partner audience, a diaspora community network, or a content partner's existing following).
- Tactics catalogue used: radio (multi-language), digital (Facebook/Instagram/YouTube), PR (influencer-seeded conversation, live radio interviews, press stories), and a **sales promo mechanic** — a "text in your details, get a small reward" database-building promo for the first N respondents.

### Phase 3 — Market content to recruit new customers (post-launch)
- Segment-by-segment content marketing: each channel gets its **own** promotional strategy and **own** line-item budget (not a shared blanket campaign) — see channel-level tactics below.
- General tactic patterns that recur across channels and are worth reusing for Asiko:
  - **Short-form social cutdowns** (60–90s) from flagship shows pushed to YouTube Shorts/Reels/TikTok
  - **Radio/media sponsorships** with opening/closing credits and in-show ad spots, for the channels where terrestrial/radio reach still matters in target markets
  - **Content-swap partnerships** with existing digital publishers (their example: Pulse) — free reach in exchange for early-window content rights
  - **Free event coverage** in exchange for brand visibility and app-download activation on-site (agents on-ground, giveaway merch)
  - **Celebrity-seeded launch content** for lifestyle/events-type channels — approach known figures to donate exclusive footage (e.g. their own weddings) to bootstrap a new channel's content library before it's commercially viable to buy content outright
  - **Presenter/talent profiling** (photoshoots, interviews) to build parasocial pull for personality-led channels
  - **Cross-promotion between channels with overlapping audiences** (their example: Foodie ↔ Nollywood channel) — worth mapping which Asiko channels share an audience so cross-promo is planned, not accidental

### Budgeting structure (template, not the actual figures)
Every channel/activity got its own mini P&L: **Production costs** (creative, jingles/spots, regulatory approval fees) + **Placement costs** (media buy, rentals, ad spend) + **Brand merchandise**, rolled up to a per-channel subtotal, and a separate stakeholder-engagement budget for the conference/launch event. For Asiko this is a clean template to build a real budget against — even at your bootstrap $2,000 POC ceiling, sizing each planned activity (content cutdown production, one social campaign, one partner outreach) against this structure will show fast where the real cost sits before you scale to more channels.

## 4. Operational content pipeline (from "Deliverables for Broadcast Team")

Separate from the marketing side, the broadcast-team deliverables email lays out a clean, dated, 9-step content operations pipeline worth adapting directly for Asiko's channel population process:

1. Catalogue all received content into a structured sheet — title, synopsis, key cast, release year, director, production house
2. Provision cloud storage sized for the ingest volume, as backup
3. Preview and quality-categorise content
4. Set up a continuous transfer pipeline to the CDN
5. Recruit reviewers specifically for content review (a distinct role from cataloguing)
6. Select previewed titles and schedule them for broadcast/delivery to the head-end (for Asiko: to Bunny Stream/Hercules)
7. Upload selected titles to cloud for download/review
8. Train staff on the ongoing content-supply-chain workflow (this isn't a one-off — it needs to run continuously)
9. Commence broadcast/go-live of channels

Every step had a **specific date attached**, all within about a two-week window — the discipline worth copying isn't the exact dates, it's that each step had one, and the plan named who owned each step. For a platform planning ~108 channels, a dated, role-owned pipeline like this per content batch is the difference between "we have content" and "content is actually scheduled and live."

## 5. Challenges, pros & cons (reading across both source projects)

### Pros of the approach (worth keeping)
- **Segment-specific marketing budgets and tactics per channel**, rather than one blanket campaign — matches Asiko's per-country/per-genre channel model well.
- **Phased go-to-market** (stakeholders → existing base → new audience) avoids trying to do everything at once.
- **Named, dated event/conference runbooks** with clear pre-requisites are genuinely reusable for the MIPAN/ADVAN/EXMAN pitch you're already planning.
- **A written content-acceptance checklist** protects curation quality once you're onboarding content fast (relevant given Blessing Agofure's 1,000-movies-in-a-month offer).
- **Explicit positioning against named competitors** for every channel forces a real differentiator instead of "we'll have good content too."

### Cons / risks (worth designing around this time)
- **The budget figures were built for a satellite/DTH-and-terrestrial-ad campaign** (₦10s–100s of millions per activity, billboard/BRT/radio spend) — none of this transfers to Asiko's bootstrap $2,000 POC economics or its digital-first, diaspora-first audience. Reuse the *structure*, not the *numbers or channel mix of tactics* (e.g., BRT bus branding and Lagos billboards are irrelevant to a UK/US/Canada diaspora audience — the old deck's own "digital marketing" and "experiential marketing" sections from the OTT deck are the more transferable half).
- **Heavy reliance on donated/free content and coverage in the early phase** (free event coverage, celebrities donating footage, free radio segments) is a real strategy but creates a dependency on goodwill and relationships that can evaporate — worth having a fallback content plan that doesn't assume free contributions land on schedule.
- **The plan assumes one content owner (a "consultant") supplies everything** — you already know from the Airband/Telairvision history how fragile that assumption is if the relationship sours or IP/attribution isn't sorted first. Any content-partner agreements for Asiko (Blessing Agofure et al.) should have the IP/attribution/payment terms nailed down *before* the content-acquisition-guidelines checklist and channel strategy get built around their material — apply the Dos/Don'ts from the earlier lessons-learned doc here directly.
- **No measurement/KPI-tracking section existed in either deck** — both plans are heavy on activity and budget, light on how success is actually measured per channel (app downloads and subscriber counts appear only at the top-level 5-year objective, not per-channel). Worth building this in for Asiko from the start, especially with 100+ channels to eventually prioritize between.
- **The conference/launch event plan assumes a single big-bang moment** — for a platform rolling out ~2 channels per country over time, a recurring, lower-cost version of that stakeholder event (e.g. quarterly or per-region) may fit better than one large one-off.
