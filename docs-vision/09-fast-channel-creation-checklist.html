How to build a FAST channel

The eleven steps to stand up a free ad-supported linear channel — and how the distribution platforms actually work.

Part 1 · The build checklist

A FAST channel is a 24/7 linear HLS stream with ad-break markers, an EPG and a brand. Whether you build it yourself or hand it to Asiko, these are the pieces.

  1. Define the channelName, genre, target audience, primary language, and the markets you want it available in. A tight editorial idea beats a broad one — "Nollywood classics," not "movies."
  2. Secure content & rights
    • Streaming (not just broadcast) rights for your territories, for the window you want.
    • Music clearance for everything in the content.
    • Either a library — 20–40+ hours to start, ideally more so it doesn't feel repetitive — or a genuine live feed for sport / news / events.
  3. Encode & package to HLSTranscode every asset to one consistent ladder — same resolutions, codec, keyframe/GOP cadence, audio. Mismatched profiles break playback at ad splice points. Output HLS (TS or CMAF). Tools: Eyevinn Encore, AWS MediaConvert, Cloudflare Stream, FFmpeg.
  4. Store it behind a CDNPut the packaged files where a playout engine and a CDN can both read them over HTTP — object storage (Bunny, R2, S3) with a CDN in front. For Asiko: Bunny.
  5. Build a schedule & EPGLay the library into a 24/7 schedule. Export it as XMLTV or MRSS — now, next, and at least 24–72 hours forward, refreshed on change. The EPG is separate from the video: it's metadata the guide reads.
  6. Run the playout
    • VOD2Live — an engine loops/schedules your files into one continuous HLS stream. Cheap, no live encoder, very reliable. Eyevinn Channel Engine does this.
    • Live encoder — only for genuine live. Needs an uplink and is fragile; always pair with a backup source and a fallback slate.
  7. Mark the ad breaksInsert SCTE-35 markers in the manifest at each break (#EXT-X-CUE-OUT/IN), or provide an EPG precise enough to derive them. Decide your pod structure — breaks per hour, slots per break, slot length. Typical FAST load: 8–14 min/hour.
  8. Prepare brand assetsChannel logo (SVG + PNG on transparent, readable on dark), an on-screen bug, a still "off-air" slate, and optional break bumpers.
  9. Choose distributionDirect to each platform, or — the practical route — one aggregator that fans your single feed out to many platforms. See Part 2.
  10. Wire up monetisationServer-side ad insertion (SSAI) against your SCTE-35, calling an ad decision server (SpringServe, Publica, Google Ad Manager) — or let the distribution platform's ad stack handle it and take a rev-share. Add house promos to backfill unsold slots.
  11. Monitor, log, reconcile24/7 monitoring with alerting, as-run logs, and a monthly reconciliation of ad-server revenue against buyer reports. This is ongoing operations, not a launch task.

With Asiko you do steps 1, 2, 5, 8 and hand us a feed (or files + schedule). Asiko does 3–4 (normalisation), 6–7 (playout & SCTE-35), 9–11 (distribution, monetisation, ops). See the List Your Channel pack and the Channel Owner Q&A.

Aggregators — the route most channel owners actually take

Integrating each platform is months of work. An aggregator takes your one feed and does the rest: format each platform's spec, handle ad ops and SSAI, deliver EPG, consolidate reporting, and pay you a single rev-share.

  • Amagi — the market leader; playout + SSAI + distribution + analytics, cloud-based.
  • Wurl (Roku-owned) — distribution + ad monetisation across CTV.
  • Frequency, Glewed TV, Zeasn/Whale — similar, various strengths.

Where Asiko sits: for African and diaspora content, Asiko is the direct, fastest route with a rev-share that isn't diluted by a chain of intermediaries. Longer term Asiko can itself be your aggregator to the global platforms, or you run Asiko alongside an aggregator for the others — listing is non-exclusive.
