2 · How they fit together

3 · Why two-sided beats a walled garden

Your own Playbook names the problem: a decade of African platforms — bank-backed, telco-backed — broke revenue-share promises. Content owners start at negative trust. A creator handing everything to a single destination is the exact fear. FOA's answer — you own your HLS, you can leave — turns that fear into the pitch. A walled garden like AVO structurally cannot offer it.

  • Platform, not just an app. FOA is picks-and-shovels for the whole market; asiko.africa is one destination on top of it.
  • Two revenue lines. Creation fees (FOA) are steady and low-risk; ad revenue (asiko.africa) scales with audience. One doesn't depend on the other.
  • Non-exclusive by design. A channel can build with FOA and also run on AVO or Roku — you still earn on the build and your own destination.

Where Asiko wins — and where not to fight

WinAfrican-owned, and it's literally true. The cap table is the differentiator. Lean on it with content owners, the diaspora, African brands, cultural bodies. AVO can't claim it.
WinThe content-owner relationship. "You keep control and can leave" is the most disarming offer to the Nollywood / creator community — and unavailable to a walled garden.
WinCategory-exclusive sponsorship. Sells differentiation to telecom, banks, fintech, remittance, travel — brands with no clean way to buy pan-African / diaspora reach today.
WinDiaspora-first. Underserved, higher CPMs, advertisers who specifically want it. Own the lane before broadening.
WinFOA powers everyone — including channels that also run on AVO. Non-exclusive. You're the infrastructure and a destination.
Don'tFight AVO on scale + free + satellite. Not winnable quickly, and the Playbook's "graveyard" warning is: don't compromise the model chasing someone else's scale.
