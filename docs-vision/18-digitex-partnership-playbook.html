01 Progress the conversation

A good verbal conversation dies the moment everyone leaves the room and remembers it differently. Your first job is to make the partner framing durable.

  • Send the recap within 24 hours. A short email to your contact restating, in your own words, that the conversation covered Media Icons Africa as Digitex's local commercial partner for Nigeria — not a software customer. Whoever writes the recap sets the frame the rest of the deal gets negotiated against.
  • Find out who actually signs. Ask directly whether the Africa lead you're speaking with can approve a partner structure, or whether it needs UK sign-off — and get that person's name. A verbal yes from someone without authority costs you weeks later.
  • Ask if a partner model already exists elsewhere. Digitex likely has a reseller or agent structure in another market (UK regional agents, or elsewhere in Africa via the "Digitex Africa" entity). A template partner already exists somewhere — get it, and you negotiate against Digitex's own precedent instead of drafting from nothing.
  • Narrow the ask to one pilot, not a market-wide deal. Propose Tattase TV, or a single FAST channel, as the proof case before any Nigeria-wide exclusivity is discussed. A big exclusive territory ask is slow to approve; a bounded pilot is fast to say yes to and builds the case for the bigger deal.
  • Get a dated next step before this goes cold. Don't let "let's keep talking" stand — name a date for the next call or the term sheet. Deals stall in the gap between meetings that don't have a next meeting attached.

02 Close the deal

Push for something signed — a Letter of Intent first if a full contract feels premature — and know your walk-away line before you're in the room negotiating it live.

Territory & exclusivity
Is Media Icons Africa the exclusive route to market in Nigeria, or one of several?
push for: exclusive, Nigeria, 12–24 month initial term
Economics
Commission or margin on software placed, vs. revenue share on ad-server/programmatic if that layer exists.
get this in writing before the pilot, not after
Client ownership
When Media Icons Africa brings a Nigerian broadcaster to Digitex, who owns that relationship going forward — you, or Digitex directly?
Minimum commitment
If exclusivity is granted, expect a minimum — deals per year or a revenue floor — attached to it.
know your realistic number before they name theirs
Your leverage
Not "we'd like to sell your software" — it's Media Icons Africa's standing relationships across Nigerian broadcast and advertiser sales, and the group's own FAST/OTT channels as a live reference site. That's what Digitex can't buy off a shelf. Anchor the negotiation there.

Before signing anything — even a lightweight LOI, have it read by counsel. A one-page review now is cheaper than an exclusivity clause you can't get out of in month eight.

03 Execute

Once terms are agreed, the work splits in two: stand up the pilot internally, and start building what you'll show the next Nigerian broadcaster you approach as Digitex's local partner.

  • Name the pilot and the owner. Pick one channel or division (Tattase TV or a FAST channel is the natural first case) and assign one person internally to see onboarding through — not a committee.
  • Get the tech and commercial teams talking directly. Whoever runs traffic/ad ops for Tattase TV should be on the integration calls with Digitex, not just you relaying messages.
  • Build the reseller pitch while the pilot runs. A short deck or one-pager positioning Media Icons Africa as Digitex's Nigerian partner is the next deliverable once terms are settled — I can put that together with you when you're ready.
