01

The infrastructure question is already answered

Before pricing desks and laptops, it's worth being explicit about what the office does not need to be — because that's the single biggest cost avoided.

No server room, no data center

Asiko TV's broadcast chain runs entirely in the cloud: Eyevinn Open Source Cloud handles playout (Channel Engine), server-side ad insertion (SGAI Pipeline + Ad Normalizer) and batch transcoding (VOD Pipeline); Bunny Stream/CDN handles storage and delivery; Hostinger hosts the front end; Hercules runs as the CMS. FOA (Fast on Africa) manages this stack for both Asiko and Homee.

So the office's "data requirement" isn't hosting anything — it's staff bandwidth to move source video up to the cloud, monitor playout remotely, and run ad campaigns in a browser. That reframes everything below: this is a lean media-ops office, not a technical facility.

03

Two build-out phases, not one

A 30-sector, 108-channel ambition doesn't need a full Lagos office on day one — it needs one by the time content and ad volume justify it. Building lean now and scaling on revenue keeps the office from becoming the thing that outruns the platform.

Phase 1 · Lean launch

Now → first 3 months

Supports the live 7-channel lineup and the Enveu build (≈10 weeks from scope sign-off)
  • Hybrid / remote-first — no fixed lease required
  • Shared serviced desks or day-passes for sponsor-facing meetings only
  • 4 laptops, 1 backup-internet kit, minimal furniture
  • Every ₦ spent is visible against the platform's own $2,000 POC ceiling — this is a separate, additional budget line
3–4people
Phase 2 · Scale-up office

Once sponsor revenue lands / rollout accelerates past 7 channels

Formal Lagos presence for advertiser credibility and larger content throughput
  • Fixed office, Victoria Island / Ikoyi or Ikeja
  • Dedicated internet + real power backup
  • Meeting room built for advertiser pitches — including video calls to diaspora buyers in the UK/US/Canada
  • 8–12 desks, dual monitors for ops/QC roles
8–12people

05

Connectivity & data

The real risk isn't bandwidth — it's Nigerian power and single-ISP outages interrupting playout monitoring or a content upload mid-transfer. Plan for redundancy, not just speed.

Provider / planPriceNote
Starlink — kit₦450,000 one-timeBest single fix for power/fibre-cut resilience; works anywhere with sky view
Starlink — Residential₦38,000–₦75,000 / mo100–200 Mbps tiers; a Priority/Business tier exists at a higher price — confirm current rate direct
Spectranet doMore₦30,000–₦60,000 / mo200–550GB fair-use cap, then throttled to ~512kbps–1Mbps — a real constraint for uploading source video
ipNX fibre (Advantage/Ultimate)₦16,000–₦158,000 / moHigher tiers give more headroom; confirm current enterprise-tier pricing, older published rates may be stale

Recommended: one fixed fibre line as primary + Starlink as automatic failover ≈ ₦90,000–₦150,000/month combined for Phase 1. Cloud storage/CDN egress (Bunny Stream) and the Eyevinn OSC usage bill are platform costs run through FOA — not an office line item, but confirm current usage-based pricing with FOA so it's budgeted somewhere.

07

Power backup

Playout itself doesn't depend on office power — it's cloud-hosted. But a mid-upload transfer or a live sponsor video call dying mid-pitch does real damage. Plan for quiet, daily continuity first, deep backup second.

SystemPriceRole
5kVA inverter (unit only)₦250,000–₦700,000Silent, automatic — covers laptops, router, lights through short outages
+ battery bank (2–4 batteries)₦300,000–₦600,000Determines how long the inverter holds without mains or fuel
3.5kVA petrol generator₦58,000–₦145,000Deep-backup for extended outages; noisier, needs fuel and attention

A serviced office (Regus-type) typically bundles power backup into the per-person fee — one more reason it's the simpler Phase-1 default.

09

90-day action plan

  1. Week 1Decide the Phase-1 footprint — remote-plus-serviced-desk vs. a small owned space. This single decision sets most of the rest of the budget.
  2. Weeks 1–2Order the 4 laptops and Starlink kit so Content Ops and Ad Ops aren't blocked while the Enveu build proceeds.
  3. Weeks 1–2Confirm Bunny Stream / Eyevinn OSC recurring costs with FOA so the real monthly platform bill is known, not estimated.
  4. Weeks 2–4Get 2–3 live quotes on serviced-desk vs. mainland coworking vs. internet plans — the figures here are planning-grade, not binding.
  5. Weeks 3–4Bring on Content Ops and Ad Ops leads — the two roles most exposed by the just-launched 7-channel lineup.
  6. Weeks 4–6Stand up the advertiser-facing meeting capability — bookable room + conferencing kit, sized for UK/US/Canada diaspora sponsor calls.
  7. OngoingRe-evaluate for Phase 2 once the first category-exclusive sponsor deals close or the channel count meaningfully exceeds 7.
